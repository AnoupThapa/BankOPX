# ADR-029 — Credit review workspace: definitions are configuration, calculations are a small fixed-point engine, nothing decides
**Status:** Accepted for Phase 6a, **pending review by the tech lead and the credit policy owner**
**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Context:** CRD-01..10 require a review workspace, not a decision engine; proposal types, checklists, calculation definitions and scorecards belong to the bank and must be approved by its credit policy owner; calculations must be deterministic, versioned and proven by bank-supplied worked examples; missing data must never be invented. We have no bank credit policy, no scorecard weights, no thresholds and no worked examples (all `[BANK-DECIDES]`).
**Decision:**
1. Four configuration kinds: `proposal_type`, `checklist`, `calculation_set`, `scorecard`. They use the existing versioned, immutable, maker-checker mechanism. Credit kinds are approved with the separate permission `config.approve_credit`, held by the `credit_policy_owner` role (high-risk role: second approver), not by the general checker.
2. **Calculations** are a tiny expression language (`ref`, `const`, `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div`) over named inputs, evaluated in BigInt fixed-point (8 internal digits, half-away-from-zero rounding). No floating point, no scripting. Division by zero is "undefined". A calculation set cannot be published unless every measure is covered by at least one worked example that reproduces exactly.
3. **Scorecards** apply bank-approved weights, bands and per-factor missing-data rules (`refer`, `exclude` and renormalise, `zero`). A `refer` factor with missing data gives "manual review" and no score. A scorecard applies to exactly one product class. Review bands can only carry review actions (`standard_review`, `enhanced_review`, `senior_review`); keys that would approve or decline are refused at publication.
4. **Proposal** per case, created by a credit officer. It pins the checklist, calculation set and scorecard versions in force at that moment and refuses mismatched product classes. Inputs, checklist answers, assessments and recommendations are append-only; each assessment stores the exact inputs used.
5. **Human judgement is separate.** The recommendation (support, support with conditions, do not support) with rationale, exceptions, mitigants, concerns and an optional score-override reason is its own record. It requires a complete required checklist and an assessment newer than the last input change.
6. **Decision stays in the workflow.** The credit module has no path that transitions a case, records an approval or creates a hand-off. Approval authority continues to use the Phase 4 approval node with `amountField`. Approval of a service with a `fulfilment` block creates the Phase 5 hand-off.
7. **Conditions** are drafted before approval, bind after approval, and are cleared with evidence by someone other than their creator (database check as well as service check).
**Consequences:** All credit logic is generic until a bank supplies definitions. The demo definitions in `credit-fixtures.ts` are synthetic and clearly marked. The language is deliberately small: no conditionals, aggregates over parties or periods, or lookups. Per-party and per-period financials are not modelled yet (A-040).
**Alternatives rejected:** shipping default weights or thresholds (invented numbers, CRD-04); a scripting language for formulas (unreviewable, non-deterministic risk); a single combined score across products (CRD-07); letting the scorecard output an approve/decline band (CRD-01).
**Verification:** `core/src/credit/credit.spec.ts`, `core/test/credit.e2e.spec.ts`, `apps/customer-portal/e2e/credit.mjs`.
